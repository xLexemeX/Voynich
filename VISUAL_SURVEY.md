# Visual survey of the manuscript (Beinecke full-resolution scans)

One entry per folio, written after looking at the page. Format: what is drawn · how the text is laid out · anything odd · links to the text statistics. Plant "identifications" are my impressions of the drawing, not botanical claims; the drawings are stylised and no plant in the manuscript has ever been identified beyond dispute.

## Quire 1–7: herbal section (Currier A unless noted)

- **f1r** — Text only, 4 paragraphs, the first three opened with big ornamental gallows. Faint green-blue leaf shapes bleed through from f1v. Red-brown "weirdo" marks at the start of paragraphs 2 and 3 and a `29`-like sign top-right (later hand). Right margin: faint later-hand alphabet. Paragraph ends are right-justified single words (`ydaraishy`, `daiin oiran`…), like signatures or refrains. Language A.

## Zodiac section (f70v–f73v), read at full resolution

Each page is a wheel: a central medallion with the sign, then one to three concentric rings of small nude or lightly dressed female figures ("nymphs"), each holding a star on a string and each with a Voynichese label beside her. Text bands run round the rings. A later hand has written the month name in Latin script inside each medallion. What I read in the medallions (my own reading of the scans, spelling as written):

| folio | sign drawn | month written | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| f70v2 | two fish head-to-tail, joined by a cord | `mars` | Pisces = March. 2 rings, ~29 nymphs, many standing in tubs/barrels |
| f70v1 | a horned goat-like ram, eating a green bush | `abril` | Aries (dark), 15 nymphs, tubs |
| f71r | a lighter ram, same pose | `aberil` / `abiril` | Aries (light), 15 nymphs. Two Aries pages of 15 = 30 |
| f71v | red-brown bull eating from a basket | `may` | Taurus (light), 15 nymphs |
| f72r1 | darker red bull, basket | `may` | Taurus (dark), 15 nymphs. Again 2 × 15 |
| f72r2 | man in green and woman in blue clasping hands | `yony` | Gemini = June. ~30 nymphs, three rings |
| f72r3 | two crayfish/lobsters, one green, one red | `jollet`(?) | Cancer = July. ~30 nymphs |
| f72v3 | a spotted feline with blue markings | `augst` | Leo = August. ~30 nymphs |
| f72v2 | a robed figure with a blue dress and cap | `septēbr` | Virgo = September |
| f72v1 | a balance with two pans | `octēbre` | Libra = October |
| f73r | a green lizard-like animal with a curled tail | `novēbre`(?) | Scorpio = November, drawn as a lizard, not a scorpion |
| f73v | a man in blue tunic with a crossbow | `decembre` | Sagittarius = December, drawn as a crossbowman, not a centaur |

Observations:
- The month names are in a **French/Occitan/Catalan-type spelling** (`abril`, `may`, `yony`, `augst`, `octembre`, `novembre`, `decembre`) written by someone other than the main scribe, in a darker, cursive later hand. `mars` for March and the `-embre` forms point to a Romance-speaking early owner. This tells us about the book's early history, not about the script.
- Pisces starts the sequence and Sagittarius ends it; Capricorn and Aquarius are missing (the folio that carried them, f74, is lost). Pisces in March and Aries in April is the *late* medieval convention (sun enters the sign in the month named), consistent with the 1404–1438 radiocarbon date.
- **Each sign gets 30 nymphs.** Pisces has ~29–30 on one page; Aries and Taurus are split over two pages of 15 each; Gemini onward have ~30 in three rings. 30 per sign = 30 degrees of the sign, or the days of a month. So the nymph labels are almost certainly **a set of 30 names per sign**: one per degree or day. That is checkable against the transcription (see NOTES §22).
- The Aries/Taurus doubling with "light" and "dark" animals looks like a deliberate split (first/second half of the month, day/night, or two decans), not an error.
- Nymphs in the early signs stand in tubs/barrels (Pisces, Aries, Taurus); from Gemini on they stand free. Some wear crowns or hats.
- The lizard for Scorpio and the crossbowman for Sagittarius match a specific northern/central European illustrated-calendar tradition (Germany/Alsace c. 1400), which is one of the few concrete art-historical handles on the book's origin.

**Section reading:** these pages are exactly the furniture of a 15th-century astronomical/astrological compendium: sun and moon faces, 12-fold, 8-fold and 28-fold wheels, phase diagrams, named star fields. The numbers 12, 8, 28 and (in the zodiac) 30 are all conventional astronomical divisions, and are the strongest evidence that the content is what it appears to be and not decoration.

**Section reading (f75–f84):** every page is water: pipes, tubs, pools, streams, canopies that rain. The nymphs are always female, often crowned, sometimes holding rings, spindles, or crosses. The only consistent story the pictures tell is *fluid moving through a system of vessels and channels*, which fits three readings the literature has offered: (a) a **balneological** treatise (medicinal baths, a real genre c. 1400), (b) a **gynaecological/anatomical** allegory (organs as pools, the Wellcome "Trotula" tradition), (c) a **cosmological allegory** of the flow of influences (the scaly clouds and rainbows point this way). What the labels give us: ~127 nymph labels, 60% starting with `o`, 40% with `ot/ok`, i.e. the *same label vocabulary as the zodiac and the jars*. The same label (`otedy`, `okol`, `okain`) appears beside a nymph, a star, and a jar. So the labels are probably not the *names* of these things (a jar is not a star) but a **shared classificatory or ordinal vocabulary** applied across the book.

**Section reading (pharma):** the format is: jar · row of labelled ingredients · paragraph, repeated. In a Latin pharmacopoeia the equivalent page would be *an electuary or compound*: the jar is the preparation, the row is its simples, the paragraph is the recipe. There are ~250 labels here and they share the `ot-/ok-` shape with the zodiac and nymph labels (50% `o`-initial, 31% `ot/ok`). If these were plant *names* I would expect the herbal's big-plant pages to use the same names, and they don't: only 19% of herbal plant labels start with `o`. So either the pharma section names *parts* or *preparations* rather than plants, or the labels are not names at all.

## "Stars" / recipes section (f103r–f116r)

- **f103r** — Text only: 19 paragraphs of 2–4 lines each, each beginning with a **star drawn in the left margin** (some painted red-brown, some plain, some with a tail), like bullet points. Folio `103`. Language B. A brown stain top right.
- **f103v** — Same format: 14 starred paragraphs, three of them long (5–6 lines). Stars are alternately red and plain.
- **f104r** — 13 starred paragraphs, of 3–4 lines each. Folio `104`. The paragraph-initial gallows is often a `p`/`f` with an elaborate loop.
- **f104v** — 15 starred paragraphs.
- **f105r** — A **double-gallows ornament** at the top ("title-like" flourish), then 12 starred paragraphs. Folio `105`.
- **f105v** — 13 starred paragraphs, several with the star coloured red.

**Section reading (stars):** this is the densest text in the book (11,600 words), all Language B, arranged as ~320 short starred entries over 23 pages. The literature calls it "recipes"; equally it could be a calendar of star-days, a prognostic (one entry per star/day), or a set of short charms. The one-star-one-paragraph layout is the same convention as the astronomical labels and matches a **star catalogue with a short text per star**. Combined with the ~300 zodiac degree labels and the 28-division wheel, an astrological reading of the *whole* book (plants → simples → baths → stars/days) is at least coherent.
